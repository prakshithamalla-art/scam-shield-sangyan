# Problem Statement

**Track A: Digital Fraud & Scam Resilience**

## India's retail investor boom
India's demat account base has grown from roughly 4 crore in early 2020 to well over 18 crore in 2025 (NSDL + CDSL data). A large share of new investors come from Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities and are first-time participants who learned about markets through social media, family and messaging apps rather than formal financial education.

## The rise of financial scams
Investment fraud now travels on the same channels first-time investors trust: WhatsApp and Telegram "tip" groups, SMS about "KYC expiry", fake trading apps shared as APK files, deepfake "expert" videos, and impersonation of banks, depositories, SEBI and the RBI. SEBI, RBI and the I4C (Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre) have all repeatedly issued public warnings about these patterns, and the national cyber-crime helpline (1930) receives a very large volume of financial-fraud complaints every year.

## Who is most vulnerable
- **First-time investors** who do not yet know what a legitimate broker, advisor or notice looks like.
- **Tier-2/Tier-3 and rural users** with less exposure to financial literacy campaigns.
- **Regional-language users.** Most warning content is in English; scam messages are increasingly in Hinglish, Hindi and Telugu.
- **Elderly and low-literacy users** who are targeted with urgency and authority ("your account will be blocked").

## Gaps in current protection
1. **Awareness is reactive.** Advisories are published after the fraud wave.
2. **Language gap.** Detection tools and warnings are mostly English-only.
3. **Complexity.** Existing checkers assume technical or financial literacy.
4. **Friction.** Reporting portals are not linked at the exact moment of doubt.
5. **Privacy fear.** People hesitate to paste real messages into tools that may store them.

## Why this matters
A single successful scam can wipe out years of savings and make a first-time investor leave the market permanently. A trusted "pause and check" step, available in the user's own language, protects both the person and confidence in the securities market.
